Book Printing Services (2026)

Short answer: a book printing service turns your print-ready files into physical books — interior, cover, binding and trim. For a standard black-and-white paperback at 500+ copies, expect roughly $2.50–$8 per copy; hardcover runs $8–$20. Short runs cost more per copy because the setup is spread over fewer books, while print-on-demand removes setup entirely and charges the most per unit.

What a printing service actually does

Printing is the last step of publishing and the one most authors underestimate. A printer is not an editor or a designer — they take finished files and produce books. A complete service covers:

  • Interior printing — text pages, on paper stock you choose (cream or white bond, 70g or 80g typical)
  • Cover printing — usually full colour on heavier stock such as 300g or 350g art card
  • Binding — perfect (paperback), case (hardcover), or saddle-stitch for thin booklets
  • Trimming — cutting to your final size, which is why the trim size must be set before layout
  • Finishing — lamination, spot UV, foil, embossing if you want them
  • Barcode and spine — your ISBN barcode on the back cover, and a spine wide enough to carry your title

Offset, digital or print-on-demand?

This choice drives your per-copy price more than anything else. It is a volume decision, not a quality one.

MethodBest forSetupTypical per copyTurnaround
Offset500+ copiesPlates per signature — paid once, every reprint$2.50–$8 paperback2–6 weeks
Digital short run50–500 copiesMinimal$5–$151–3 weeks
Print-on-demand1–50 copies, testingNone$8–$20+3–5 days per order

The trap: offset plates cannot be reused. Printing 500 now and 500 later pays for the plates twice, so a split run usually costs more overall than ordering the full quantity once. If you are confident of demand, order up; if you are not, start digital and accept the higher unit price as the price of uncertainty.

What moves your price

  • Quantity — the single biggest factor; setup is spread across every copy
  • Page count — more signatures, more plates, more paper, and thicker binding
  • Trim size — an A4 page uses roughly twice the paper of A5 for the same page count
  • Colour — four-colour printing on every page is several times the price of black text
  • Paper stock — heavier and coated stocks cost more per ream and ship heavier
  • Binding and finish — hardcover, foil and special finishes add real money

How to choose a printing partner

  • Ask for a proof before the full run — a digital proof at minimum, a physical one if the run is large
  • Send a template — the printer should supply the exact page size and margin guidance before you lay out
  • Confirm what happens on a reprint — plates are charged again, so ask for the reprint price up front
  • Check the barcode — the ISBN barcode must sit on the cover file, correctly sized, on a light background
  • Get delivery in writing — including who pays and what happens if the books arrive damaged

Common mistakes

  • Laying out the book before confirming the trim size and paper
  • Forgetting that a thin book has no spine — under about 60 pages, cover text has nowhere to go
  • Choosing A4 because it feels roomier, then paying double for paper
  • Ordering a split run and paying for plates twice
  • Accepting a screenshot as a proof instead of a signed-off file

Frequently asked questions

How many copies should I print?

Print what you can realistically sell or distribute within a year — typically 100–500 for a first book. Per-copy price falls sharply up to about 1,000 copies, then flattens.

Can I print just one copy?

Yes, through print-on-demand. It is the most expensive per copy but involves no setup and no stock, which makes it ideal for a proof or a first sale.

Do you need my ISBN to print?

For retail distribution, yes — the barcode is generated from the ISBN and placed on the cover. Printing without one is possible for private or family copies.

How long does printing take?

Offset is typically 2–6 weeks depending on quantity and queue; digital short runs 1–3 weeks; print-on-demand days per order.

Is printing cheaper in another country?

Sometimes per unit, but freight, duty and returns usually erase the difference. Print where you sell.
