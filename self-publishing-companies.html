Self-Publishing Companies (2026)

Short answer: a self-publishing company handles the work you would otherwise do alone — editing, cover, interior layout, ISBN, printing and distribution — for a fee, while you keep your rights and your royalties. Expect roughly $699–$9,999 depending on scope. The model only works in your favour if the ISBN stays yours, the royalty stays 100%, and you receive actual printed books.

What you are actually paying for

  • Editing and proofreading — developmental, line or copy editing depending on the manuscript
  • Cover design — from a basic template to a fully custom illustration
  • Interior layout — trim size, typography, front and back matter
  • ISBN and barcode — the identifier retail needs, plus the barcode artwork
  • Printing and binding — physical copies, usually a set number included
  • Distribution setup — Amazon KDP, IngramSpark, Apple, Kobo, and library channels
  • eBook conversion — EPUB for Kindle and other stores
  • Launch support — listing optimisation, creatives, sometimes advertising

The three models — and which one you are being sold

ModelWhat it meansWho owns whatTypical spend
DIYYou use free tools and platforms yourselfYou own everything; you do the work$0–$500
Service companyYou pay a fee; they deliver services; you keep rightsYou own ISBN, rights, 100% royalty$699–$9,999
Hybrid / partnershipThey invest, take a share, or hold rightsOften their ISBN, shared royaltyVaries — read the contract carefully

The middle column is the whole decision. A fee for services is fine. A permanent claim on your rights or royalties is not the same product, however similar the sales pitch sounds.

Typical price bands

  • Entry (about $699) — cover, layout, ISBN, submission to the platforms, no printed copies
  • Mid (about $1,299) — adds printed copies, eBook conversion, print-on-demand distribution
  • Full (about $2,499) — adds professional copyediting and proofreading, more copies, social creatives, listing optimisation
  • Premium (about $4,999) — developmental review, hardcover options, author website, launch marketing
  • Legacy/executive (about $9,999) — ghostwriting, large print runs, PR, audiobook, launch events

Seven red flags

  • The ISBN is registered in their name — then they control your book, not you
  • They take a percentage of royalties — you should keep 100% under a service model
  • No printed copies included at a price that implies them
  • Vague scope — "editing" without saying which kind, or how many words
  • Pressure and deadlines — "this price today only" is a sales tactic, not a business constraint
  • No proof stage — nobody should go to press without your approval of the final files
  • They will not put it in writing — agreement first, payment second, always

DIY or a company?

DIY makes sense if you have time, a design instinct and no deadline. A company makes sense when the book is a business asset — a lead generator, a credential, a legacy — and the opportunity cost of doing it yourself exceeds the fee. The honest test: what is the book worth to you if it releases next quarter instead of next year?

How to choose

  • Ask what happens to your ISBN, rights and royalty in writing
  • Ask how many printed copies are included, and at what specification
  • Ask who edits, and see a sample of their editing before you commit
  • Ask for a proof and a printed copy before the main run
  • Ask what the total is — not the entry price

Frequently asked questions

Do I keep my rights?

Under a service model, yes — you own the copyright, the ISBN and 100% of royalties. Under a hybrid or partnership model, check the contract: rights and royalties are exactly where those deals differ from a service.

How long does it take?

Typically 6–12 weeks from final manuscript to printed books, depending on editing depth and print method.

Can I publish on Amazon myself instead?

Yes, and it costs nothing. What you are buying from a company is the editing, design, ISBN, printing and coordination — not access to Amazon.

Is a cheaper package always worse?

No — if your manuscript is already edited and you only need layout, ISBN and submission, paying for a full edit is waste. Match the package to what your book actually needs.

What does a first-time author usually need?

Proofreading at minimum, a professional cover, correct layout, an ISBN with barcode, and a print run matched to realistic demand. Marketing is the step most authors under-budget and most need.
