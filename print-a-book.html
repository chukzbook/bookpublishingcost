Print a Book (2026)

Short answer: to print a book you need print-ready interior and cover files, and then you choose how many copies to run. One copy is possible through print-on-demand; genuine per-copy economy starts around 500 copies, where a black-and-white paperback lands near $2.50–$8 a copy. Offset printing takes 2–6 weeks; digital short runs 1–3 weeks.

The five steps

  • 1. Set the specification — trim size, page count, paper, binding and finish. Everything downstream depends on this, and changing it later means redoing the layout
  • 2. Prepare print-ready files — interior as a PDF with embedded fonts, correct margins and bleed; cover as a separate PDF including spine and barcode
  • 3. Proof — approve a digital proof, and a physical one for larger runs. This is the last cheap moment to catch an error
  • 4. Print and bind — offset for volume, digital for short runs, print-on-demand for single copies
  • 5. Deliver — confirm who receives the stock, and where

Print-ready files: the checklist

  • Trim size matches what you ordered — exactly
  • Fonts embedded, images at 300dpi or higher
  • Margins: at least 15mm inside for a perfect-bound book, more for thick books
  • Bleed of 3mm on any image or colour that runs to the edge
  • Page count in multiples of 4 — books are printed in folded sections
  • Cover file includes back, spine and front as one spread, with the ISBN barcode placed and sized
  • Spine width calculated from your page count and paper stock, not guessed

Two things surprise authors here. First, plates cannot be reused: a 500-then-500 split pays for them twice, so splitting a run usually costs more than ordering it whole. Second, per-copy price flattens after about 1,000 copies, so ordering 3,000 to "save money" usually just buys storage.

What changes your price

  • Page count and trim — an A4 page uses about twice the paper of A5
  • Colour — four colour throughout is several times the price of black text
  • Paper stock — cream, white, coated art paper and heavier weights all price differently
  • Binding — perfect binding is standard; case binding costs more
  • Finishing — lamination, foil and embossing are charged per copy

Timeline

Allow 2–6 weeks for offset printing once files are approved, 1–3 weeks for digital short runs, and days for print-on-demand. Add delivery time, and for international shipping add customs. If you have a launch date, work backwards from it rather than forwards from today.

Common mistakes

  • Laying out before confirming the trim and paper
  • Guessing the spine width — it is arithmetic, and getting it wrong wastes the whole cover run
  • Approving a screenshot instead of a signed-off proof
  • Ordering more copies than you can distribute
  • Forgetting that a thin book has no spine for text

Frequently asked questions

Can I print a book without an ISBN?

Yes, for private or family copies. For retail, you need one — the barcode on the cover is generated from it.

Is it cheaper to print abroad?

Sometimes per unit, but freight and duty usually remove the advantage. Print close to where you sell.

How much does it cost to print one book?

Print-on-demand typically runs $8–$20+ for a paperback depending on page count and trim — far more per copy than a bulk run, but with no setup and no stock.

Do I need a proof?

Always. A digital proof catches text and layout errors; a physical proof catches colour and paper problems that screens hide.

What if the printed books are defective?

Printing defects are the printer's responsibility — get the defect and reprint policy in writing before you order, including the window in which you can claim.
